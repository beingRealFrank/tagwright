We scanned the PDFs on 33 local government sites

3,635 PDFs, 33 town, city and county websites. Here is what a typical site actually has to fix before the ADA Title II dates.

Between November 13 and 16, 2026 we ran our free triage scan over a list of US town, city and county websites. The scan follows each site's links, opens every PDF it finds and sorts it into a bucket. 33 of the sites had PDFs we could reach (the others had none we could find, did not resolve, or blocked automated visits). This is a small sample that we chose from a public list of .gov domains, so treat it as a picture, not a statistic.

The useful finding: the pile is smaller than it looks

Only about one PDF in nine was current and untagged. Over a fifth looked old enough to be archive candidates, and another 7% looked like they could simply be deleted. The median site had 4 PDFs worth remediating, about 14 pages. At our prices (1 credit a page, $1.00 to $1.50) that is under $30 for the median site. A few sites were far bigger, and the average is pulled up to about $230 by two large sites with thousands of pages.

What to do with this

  1. Inventory your PDFs before you buy anything; our checker will list up to 60 at a time in your browser.
  2. Delete what you no longer need, and move true reference material to a clearly marked archive. Read what the rule says about archived content first.
  3. Fix the current documents people use to take part in your services first.
  4. Find your own deadline: April 26, 2027 for 50,000 people or more, April 26, 2028 for smaller governments.

The bucket labels are an automated guess from each file's date, tags, page count and layout. They are a starting list for a person, not a legal finding. Want the list for your own site? Tell us the site or run the free check on a few files first.
