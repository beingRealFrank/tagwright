ADA Title II and your PDFs

State and local governments must make web content, including PDFs, meet WCAG 2.1 AA. The deadlines moved, but the work did not get smaller.

The dates

Find your own date with the deadline calculator.

The Department of Justice's 2024 rule under Title II of the ADA adopted WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the technical standard for state and local government web content and mobile apps. On April 20, 2026 the DOJ published an interim final rule that pushed the compliance dates back a year:

  • April 26, 2027 for public entities serving 50,000 people or more;
  • April 26, 2028 for smaller public entities and special district governments.

The technical standard did not change, and Title II's general duty not to discriminate applies today regardless of the rule's dates. Check the Federal Register for anything newer before you plan around these dates.

PDFs are web content

The rule covers "conventional electronic documents": PDFs, word-processing files, presentations and spreadsheets that you make available on your site. For a PDF, WCAG 2.1 AA in practice means: the file is tagged, the reading order is logical, headings and lists are marked up, images have text alternatives, tables have header cells, form fields have names, the language is set, and text is real text rather than a scanned picture.

Not every old PDF has to be fixed

The rule has exceptions that matter a great deal for a large document library. Two of them are:

  • Archived web content: content created before the compliance date, kept only for reference, research or recordkeeping, not changed since it was archived, and kept in a clearly marked archive area.
  • Preexisting conventional electronic documents: PDFs and similar files posted before the compliance date, unless they are currently used to apply for, get access to, or take part in your services, programs or activities. A current application form, fee schedule or permit guide is not exempt just because it is old.

Exceptions also cover some third-party content, individualized password-protected documents and preexisting social media posts. Each has conditions: read the rule text, and when in doubt, ask your counsel. Exceptions don't remove the duty to give an accessible version to a person who asks for one.

Triage before you remediate

Most public sites have hundreds or thousands of PDFs. Remediating all of them at human-shop prices ($5 to $25 a page) is rarely sensible. A better order:

  1. Inventory every PDF your site links to, with its date, page count and where it is linked from.
  2. Delete what is out of date and no longer needed.
  3. Archive what qualifies, in a clearly labelled archive section, unchanged.
  4. Convert to HTML short, frequently used content (opening hours, contact pages, simple notices). HTML is easier to keep accessible than PDF.
  5. Remediate what remains, starting with forms and documents people need to use your services.
