A PDF accessibility plan for a small town

Under 50,000 people means a later date, April 26, 2028, not a smaller job. A plan that fits a part-time web person and a small budget.

Under the Department of Justice's 2026 interim final rule, public entities serving fewer than 50,000 people, and special district governments, have until April 26, 2028 to meet WCAG 2.1 AA for their web content, including PDFs. Larger entities have until April 26, 2027. Check the Federal Register for anything newer before you plan around the dates. This is not legal advice.

1. Count before you spend (week 1)

List every PDF your site links to: where it is linked from, its date and its page count. In our scan of 33 local government sites (full results) most of the pile was not the problem: 35% of the PDFs were already tagged, 22% looked old enough to be archive candidates and only about 11% were current and untagged. The median site had about 4 PDFs, 14 pages, to fix.

2. Cut the pile (weeks 2 to 4)

  • Delete PDFs that are out of date and not required to keep.
  • Move reference material that fits the archived-content exception into a clearly labelled archive section, unchanged (what the rule requires).
  • Turn short, popular PDFs (hours, fee schedules, contact lists) into web pages.

3. Fix what people use (months 2 to 6)

Start with forms, applications, agendas and anything residents need to use a service. Fix new documents at the source, with real headings and styles in Word or Google Docs (example for agendas), and treat remediation of finished PDFs as the exception.

4. Keep a record

For each file keep the date checked, the validator result and what still needed a person. That is the paper trail an auditor or a resident's complaint will ask for.
