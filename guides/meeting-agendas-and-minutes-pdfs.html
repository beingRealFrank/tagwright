Meeting agendas and minutes as PDFs

The documents a town posts most often are also the ones residents use to take part. Here is how the Title II rule reads for them, and a workflow that avoids fixing them one at a time.

The Department of Justice's ADA Title II web rule treats PDFs and other files on your site as web content. It has an exception for older "preexisting" documents, but that exception does not apply to documents that are currently used to apply for, get access to, or take part in a public entity's services, programs or activities. We read an agenda for an upcoming meeting as that kind of document: residents use it to take part. Whether a particular file is exempt is a legal question for your counsel; this page is not legal advice.

Why agendas and minutes are the hard case

  • They are posted again every meeting, so a one-time cleanup of the backlog does not help.
  • They are often assembled from several sources: a Word agenda, scanned signature pages, attached reports.
  • Scanned or "printed to PDF" packets have no tags and sometimes no real text at all, so a screen reader reads nothing.

A workflow that scales

  1. Fix the source, not the PDF. Write the agenda in Word or Google Docs using real heading and list styles, add a document title and language, then export to PDF with "document structure tags" turned on. That gives most of the tagging for free.
  2. Put the agenda text on the page too. An HTML agenda is easiest to keep accessible, and the PDF becomes a copy.
  3. Avoid scanning signed pages into the main file. If you must, send them as a separate attachment or run OCR so the text can be read.
  4. Check each file before posting. A free browser check shows whether the file is tagged, has a title and a language, and has any text. It does not look at the content for you.
  5. Remediate the leftovers in batches. For documents that come out of the process untagged, pay per page rather than per project.
