How it works

  1. Prepare. Scanned pages get OCR; missing fonts get embedded.
  2. Tag. Layout analysis builds the structure: headings, paragraphs, lists, tables with header cells, figures, links, and a logical reading order. Page furniture becomes artifacts.
  3. Describe. Each figure gets alt text, and a second, independent check compares it with the image. Disagreements are flagged, not hidden.
  4. Verify. Every output file runs through veraPDF, the open-source PDF/UA validator. Language, title and metadata are set.
  5. Prove. You get an evidence pack: the veraPDF report, a checkpoint table marking each item machine-verified, automated review or human-sampled, an alt-text manifest, a table-header manifest, a reading-order transcript, SHA-256 hashes of input and output, and a signed ledger entry.

On a test of 150 real US government PDFs, 83% passed all three of our automated checks: the veraPDF PDF/UA-1 validator, no text lost, and the text covered by the tags. 66% were also fully ready, meaning every image description passed an independent automated review; in the rest, descriptions are flagged for a person to check. The rest are reported with exactly what failed, most often a font that could not be embedded or a table the layout analysis could not read. We have not had image descriptions rated by people.

Machine checks are necessary, not sufficient. That is why every evidence pack says which checks a machine made, which an automated review made, and which still need a person. Files whose results we cannot stand behind are held back for a manual check instead of being delivered.

Questions

Does a fixed PDF satisfy the ADA, Section 504 or the European Accessibility Act?

Those laws apply to your whole service, so no single file or tool can satisfy them on its own, and we do not claim it can. What a fixed file gives you is evidence you can point to: tags for headings, lists, tables and reading order, a description for every image, the open-source veraPDF validator's result for that file, and a line-by-line record of which checks a machine made and which still need a person. That is the paper trail an auditor, a complainant or your own accessibility lead will ask for. This is not legal advice.

What happens to my files?

The free check never uploads them. Files you send for fixing are processed by an automated pipeline, are never used to train models, and are deleted 30 days after delivery. Please don't send health records or student records.

Why not just use an auto-tag button?

One-click tagging is cheap but unchecked: you can't see what it guessed. Human remediation shops are thorough but cost $5 to $25 a page. Tagwright sits between: automated, cross-checked, and documented page by page.

Can we pay with a purchase card, and do we get a receipt?

Yes. Credit packs are paid by card at checkout, and the receipt that arrives by email can serve as your invoice. If your office needs a W-9 for a larger order, email us the amount.

What still needs a person after you fix a file?

Anything a machine cannot judge: whether an image description says what matters, whether the reading order makes sense, and whether headings and tables match your intent. Each evidence pack lists which checks a machine made, which an automated review made, and which still need a person. Files we cannot stand behind are held for a manual check, not delivered.

Is the free sample really free?

Yes: upload one PDF without a license key and we fix its first 3 pages, with the same report and evidence pack. No sign-up and no card.

Can you fix Word or PowerPoint files?

PDF first. DOCX, PPTX and EPUB are on the way.
